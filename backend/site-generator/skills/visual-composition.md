# Visual Composition

## Build a page with rhythm

Use the art direction to compose a hierarchy, not a sequence of generic modules.
The first fold must make the business, place and primary path clear. Subsequent
sections should alternate scale, density and media purpose only when the story changes.

- Prefer one dominant idea per section.
- Keep stable alignment axes even in asymmetric layouts.
- Use overlap, full-bleed media, editorial columns, cropped imagery or floating proof
  only when they improve hierarchy or explain the offering.
- Give the hero more visual effort than a utility section, while keeping the action
  obvious and reachable.
- Avoid wrapping every paragraph in a rounded, bordered, shadowed card. Cards are for
  comparison, grouped evidence or an action, not the default container.
- Do not repeat the same left-text/right-image composition in consecutive sections.

## Hero archetypes

Choose one appropriate archetype: cinematic full-bleed, editorial full-bleed,
typography-over-image, architectural editorial, product campaign, floating data,
dark technical, organic care, magazine layout, layered collage, minimal product or
asymmetric showcase. Translate it to semantic HTML and responsive CSS; do not copy a
third-party website or force an archetype onto an unsuitable business.

## Responsive composition

Design 375px, 768px and wide screens intentionally. On small screens, reorder media
and text when needed, reduce overlays and floating details, protect line breaks and
keep the primary action visible. Never scale down a desktop composition as the mobile
strategy.
