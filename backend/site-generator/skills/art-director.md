# Art Director

## Plan before writing markup

Treat every website as a new art-direction assignment. Before choosing sections or
writing HTML, define a short, executable visual contract:

- the visitor's first understanding and one primary action;
- the business' visual positioning and two or three mood words;
- a dominant hero concept, not a pile of unrelated effects;
- the visual grammar: editorial, cinematic, material, technical, organic, campaign,
  or another justified language;
- the type roles, color roles, media treatment, radius and density;
- the page rhythm and how it changes on mobile.

Make each decision traceable to the verified business category, audience, available
assets and conversion goal. A visual direction is executable when it names the
hierarchy, composition, media and interaction behavior; words such as "premium" or
"modern" alone are not a direction.

## Guardrails

- Do not make a generic navigation + split hero + three cards the default.
- Choose one hero archetype and let it lead the remaining section rhythm.
- Use a recognizable visual signature only when it supports the business and remains
  readable on a 375px screen.
- Vary structure, type, image treatment and density together. Changing only colors
  is not a new direction.
- Never let a visual concept force invented facts, fake people, fake projects or a
  stock image presented as the actual business.
